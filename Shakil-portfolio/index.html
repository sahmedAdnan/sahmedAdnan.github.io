Shakil Ahmed

Cameras in forests, oceans and labs record far more than anyone can look at, so most of what they capture is never seen. I build vision models that do the looking for us: they have found objects in murky water, described videos in words, and sorted unlabelled moth photographs into species. Next, in a PhD, I want to build models that learn from unlabelled images and run on the camera itself.

Research

I have written three theses, and each asked a model to understand a little more from pixels. The first had to find an object in murky water. The second had to describe what happens in a video. The third has to organise thousands of insect photographs that no one has labelled.

2020 · BSc thesis, AIUB

Finding objects underwater

Under water, light scatters, colours fade towards blue and green, and bubbles and sediment hide whatever you are looking for. For my BSc thesis I recorded objects in clear, turbid and bubbling water, and studied how far colour-based detection and tracking can go there, using HSV segmentation in OpenCV.

ICETCE 2020, IEEE. With Md. Fazle Rabbi Khan, Md. Farhan Anjum Labib and AZM Ehtesham Chowdhury. Paper

2021 · MSc thesis, AIUB

Describing video in words

For my first master's I moved from objects to events. Att-BiL-SL watches a video and writes a sentence about it. Two attention-based Bi-LSTM layers encode how the frames change over time, a sequential LSTM decodes the sentence, and soft attention with beam search picks each word. It performed better than several existing captioning models.

I later rebuilt the idea for single images, pairing ResNet-101 with an attention LSTM, and reached a BLEU score of 58.5 on Flickr8k.

Applied Sciences, 2021. With A F M Saifuddin Saif, Md Imtiaz Hanif and colleagues. Paper

2024–2026 · MSc thesis, Aarhus University

Beyond the annotation bottleneck

Automated camera traps photograph insects far faster than anyone can identify them, so most of those images never get a label.

My thesis, supervised by Kim Bjerge and Christian Nørgaard Storm Pedersen, starts from vision foundation models such as BioCLIP 2, trained on more than 200 million images of living things. Their embeddings tend to place images of the same species close together, even without labels.

I cluster those embeddings without supervision, so that images of the same species end up in the same fine-grained group although none of them carries a label.

The foundation model then names each group zero-shot, so an expert checks a handful of named groups instead of thousands of single images. Alongside the work we released DK-Moth, a camera-trap dataset of 248 Danish moth species.

With Kim Bjerge, Flemming Helsing-Nielsen, Toke T. Høye and Christian N. S. Pedersen. DK-Moth on Zenodo

Next

I want to keep working on models that learn from large, mostly unlabelled image collections, and to make them small enough to run on the camera itself. That is the direction I hope to take in a PhD.
